Unable to open the hinge, she resigned herself back to her indifference and threw it into the bottom drawer of her hand-me-down yellow filling cabinet.

Like every night, she prepared for sleep by changing from her drab human costume into the only clothing item she actually loved: Her red and white striped pajamas. She'd found them in the bottom of an amoire in Uncle Chester's old antique shop years ago and immediately fell in love. The fabric had a unique smoothness, like the velure covers on some higher-end DVD collections. They made her feel like Waldo, or Nemo. A dreamer who still wanted to be found.

She had wore those pajamas every night since, without fail and tonight was no different. Wrapped up tight, she climbed under the jade green covers and stared up at the ceiling. The room was painted in a liminal almost-dark, city lights pushing in a timeless glow against her empty walls. She thought about the antique shop, about how little she remembered of herself.

"It's the trauma," her therapist had told her. "Memory loss and focus issues at this level are highly common in persons with complex backgrounds such as yourself."

She wondered what complex background might look like. She couldn't remember. What if it wasn't all that complex?

The deeper she looked to remember something, the heavier her mind fell. Not long after, sleep came after her.

That night, she dreamed of the Cabin.
