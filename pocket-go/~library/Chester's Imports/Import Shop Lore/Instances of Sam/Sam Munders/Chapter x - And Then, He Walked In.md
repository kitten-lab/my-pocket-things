The bell above the door rustled, the harmonic and yet chaotic windchime mechanism singing its announcement song for the second time today. Sam's eyes lifted from the stack of papers she'd found under the bathroom sink, brow furrowed. 

She half expected Aunt May's ghoulish form to darken the doorway again, a haunting and chilling experience she was not eager to repeat. 

Instead, there was *him*.

Golden hour-- the light pooled in from the small building's windows and glass door, which now banged shut behind him, the jingling chimes offering a cacophony to match the breath caught in Sam's throat.  Light entering naturally was rare at the shop, only at sundown, when the light would catch the only side of the shop which was now shadowed by the mega-buildings which flank it. 

And now light entered as if he himself had brought it here. 
Whoever *he* was.

He did not look to the back of the shop right away, and she took the opportunity to slide half behind the wall separating the front and back of the shop. She wasn't sure why she didn't want to be seen, but something about the way the air shifted as he entered frazzled her. 

She wanted to measure him up, understand who this stranger was who'd entered Uncle Chester's -- *her* -- shop today. She'd been here for two weeks, and only the usual suspects ever arrives: Aunt May, Beverly, and Archer.

He appeared to be around her age, with long dark waves set against sun-kissed skin. The pooling light echoed evidence that this man was well loved by the sun, its light glittering and glinting off the edges of him as his eyes swept the perimeter of the shop. His fingers brushed back the thick locks from his face, revealing equally dark features. The vision shocked her -- this man was gorgeous, who was he?  A wave of heat shifted warm in her chest. She shifted further behind the wall.

He moved like fluid moves: gentle, sensual, fingers touching the stacks of old books and knickknacks nearest to the door as he stepped slowly into the threshold of the shop. 

"Hello?" the stranger called into the space. His voice was deep, rumbling. Her skin prickled, breath quickened. She shifted, hid herself completed, and pressed her hands to her mouth as if she needed to silence herself from reply. 

"Anyone here?" he called out again, having heard no reply.  "Huh," he muttered under his breath, and she half expected he would turn around and leave, but the sound of the chimes did not happen again. After a few moments, her intrigue overpowered her better judgement, and she peered out into the shop once more.

He was wearing all black, his shirt buttoned up only half way, a dangle of mala beads pressed against his chest. She found herself unconsciously biting her lip as her eyes drank in the entire form of him. He was tall, at least 6'3", shoulders set wide apart, strong chest. She could tell he worked out, and found herself equally intoxicated and frustrated with her own intoxication. This was not the sort of distraction she had been planning to fall inside of.

He walked slowly through the center of the shop, the only area with any sense of clear footpath. His hand lazily brushed the surfaces as he passed them, eyes moving across each unique piece. The light seemed to pull along with him, every step he took bathed in glimmer and wonder. She rubbed her eyes, the effect so uncanny she wondered if somehow she was imagining it.

The way he moved in the shop, she sensed he had been here before. He flipped over an old wood board, the underside of which was an arcanely designed board game even Uncle Chester didn't know the original name of. He tapped the center of it three times with his index finger, traced a spiral out from the center of the board to its far edges. 

She swallowed hard watching. Chester always done the same, every time they had played. How did he know to do that? How did he even know the game board was *there*? She certainly didn't recognize him. He was so beautiful, she would have remembered. She knew she would have remembered.

"Chester?" his voice echoed out once more. Sam's heart dropped: he knew Chester. He knew this place. The recognition split inside of her between the ache of losing the only father figure she ever had, and the brewing curiosity to know who this strange man was, and perhaps what he remembered of her uncle.
