    The Rose walked down the salted road, the sterile terrain of expectation and discourse, a lone echo of a Self long since left in the shallows. Her hands grip the strap of the old canvas bag, warn down by the miles of walking in the bleak sun the world called Hope. The Rose knew Hope once, Icarus to the sun, burned the petals, singed the wings.
    
    A canal formed the endless streak of duty, a line she once believed was the imprint of the Summer River, now a painful drought carved into the imaginal sand of the nowhere she endless belonged. A long road, a forgotten road, a road which led beyond so many mirages, or were they prophecies? Her lips dry, stems left out in the cold brittle air. A memory, how does she come home to the place she remembered herself before?

--

    Freedom long since lost its legs in the war. Face contorted by the forward-faced requirements, unpaid debts, unpaid memories. There was no pressure to live here, and yet Freedom could not escape, could not breathe, clamped down with the illusion of what used to be ankles and toes, bones and breath.
